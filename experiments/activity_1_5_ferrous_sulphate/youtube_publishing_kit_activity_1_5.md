### 2. Copy-Paste YouTube Video Description - 100 Percent Bracket-Free

Why does your chemistry teacher warn you never to inhale directly from this boiling tube? Watch the dramatic real lab demonstration of CBSE Class 10 Science Activity 1.5: Thermal Decomposition of Ferrous Sulphate Crystals!

When pale green crystals of ferrous sulphate heptahydrate - green vitriol - are heated in a dry boiling tube:
First, the crystals lose their water of crystallisation and turn dirty white, causing droplets of liquid water to condense on the cooler upper inside walls of the tube.

On strong heating, the anhydrous ferrous sulphate decomposes into a dark reddish-brown solid residue of Ferric Oxide, Fe2O3, releasing pungent, suffocating fumes of sulfur dioxide, SO2, and sulfur trioxide, SO3, gases with the characteristic choking smell of burning sulfur!

Balanced Chemical Equation:
2FeSO4 solid → Fe2O3 solid + SO2 gas + SO3 gas
Ferrous Sulphate Green → Ferric Oxide Reddish-Brown + Sulfur Oxides

Key Class 10 Board Exam Viva Takeaways:
1. Type of Reaction: Thermal Decomposition Reaction - a single reactant breaks down into three simpler products upon heating.
2. Color Change: Pale green crystals turn dirty white, then dark reddish-brown.
3. Characteristic Smell: Suffocating choking odor of burning sulfur due to SO2 and SO3 gases.
4. NCERT Lab Safety Rule: Never point the mouth of the boiling tube towards yourself or neighbors; gently waft the fumes towards your nose with your hand.
