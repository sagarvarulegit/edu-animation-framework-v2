# Lesson Script: Refraction of Light Through a Triangular Glass Prism

**Subject**: Physics  
**Grade / Level**: CBSE Class 10 (Chapter 10: Light & Chapter 11: Human Eye and Colourful World)  
**Experiment**: NCERT Activity 10.1 / 11.2 — Refraction through a Glass Prism  

---

## Pedagogical Overview

A triangular glass prism has two triangular bases and three rectangular lateral refracting surfaces. Unlike a rectangular glass slab where the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray (suffering only lateral displacement), in a prism the non-parallel refracting faces cause the emergent ray to bend significantly towards the base of the prism, subtending a characteristic **Angle of Deviation ($D$)** with the incident ray.

---

## Scene 01 — Apparatus & Setup

**Voiceover Narration**:  
> "To study the refraction of light through a prism, we fix a sheet of white paper on a wooden drawing board using thumb pins. We place an equilateral glass prism on the paper and trace its boundary outline $ABC$ with a sharp pencil. The angle between its two lateral refracting faces is the Angle of the Prism, denoted as $\angle A$ ($60^\circ$)."

**Key Visuals**:  
- Wooden drawing board + white paper + 4 corner push pins.  
- Transparent equilateral optical glass prism placed in the center.  
- Highlighting Apex Angle $A = 60^\circ$.  

---

## Scene 02 — Incident Ray & First Refraction

**Voiceover Narration**:  
> "We direct a narrow beam of light along the line $PE$, making an Angle of Incidence $i$ with the normal $N_1N_1'$ drawn perpendicular to face $AB$. We fix two optical pins $P_1$ and $P_2$ vertically on this line. As light enters the denser glass medium from the rarer air, it slows down and bends towards the normal along path $EF$, with an Angle of Refraction $r_1$."

**Key Formulas**:  
- Snell's Law at face $AB$: $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r_1} = n = 1.517$  
- Angle of incidence $i = 48^\circ \implies r_1 = 29.3^\circ$.  

---

## Scene 03 — Emergence & Angle of Deviation

**Voiceover Narration**:  
> "At the second face $AC$, the ray travels from the optically denser glass into the rarer air. It bends away from the second normal $N_2N_2'$ along path $FS$ with an Angle of Emergence $e$. Looking through face $AC$, we place pins $P_3$ and $P_4$ so they appear in line with the images of $P_1$ and $P_2$. Extending the incident ray $PE$ forward and the emergent ray $FS$ backward, they meet at point $G$. The angle $\angle HGF$ between the incident direction and the emergent ray is the **Angle of Deviation ($D$)**."

**Key Formulas**:  
- $\angle A + \angle D = \angle i + \angle e$  
- $60^\circ + 38.7^\circ = 48^\circ + 50.7^\circ = 98.7^\circ$ (Verified).  

---

## Scene 04 — Dispersion into Rainbow Spectrum

**Voiceover Narration**:  
> "When a composite white light beam passes through the prism, each color component travels at a different speed through the glass medium. Violet light has the shortest wavelength and highest refractive index, so it deviates the most. Red light has the longest wavelength and lowest refractive index, so it deviates the least. This splitting of white light into its seven constituent colors (VIBGYOR) on the observation screen is called **Dispersion**."

**Spectrum Sequence**:  
- **V** — Violet ($400\text{ nm}$, maximum deviation)  
- **I** — Indigo  
- **B** — Blue  
- **G** — Green  
- **Y** — Yellow  
- **O** — Orange  
- **R** — Red ($700\text{ nm}$, minimum deviation)
